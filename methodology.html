Methodology

Six ranking models, in two groups by the question they answer. Each is fit from game results and run for both divisions.

  • Strength of record NPI · KRACH · Massey · HockeyBT
  • Current strength ELO · Dynamic
  • Also Model settings · Conference strength

How to read these rankings

Strength of record ranks teams on what they have done this season, with every game counting the same. Current strength estimates how strong a team is now, so recent results count more and earlier seasons can inform it.

Each division's rankings page shows the models it runs as tabs over the same team list, grouped as below. The groups answer different questions, so they won't always agree, and neither is the “right” one. Within a group the models differ in inputs and mechanics: some fit all of the season's games at once (Massey, KRACH, HockeyBT), some update as results arrive (ELO, Dynamic), and one is a fixed formula (NPI). Some use goals scored, and others only wins and losses.

Strength of record

These answer “how good are this season's results?” Every game counts the same, whenever it was played, and nothing from earlier seasons is used. They suit comparing resumes, as a selection committee does.

NPI

Men'sWomen's

A reimplementation of the NCAA Power Index (NPI), the formula the NCAA selection committee uses.

How it works

Combines a team's winning percentage (road wins weighted more than home wins) with strength of schedule, measured by opponents' winning percentage, and a bonus for wins over strong opponents, into a single index.

Main attributes

  • Formula, not a fitted model
  • Wins and losses only
  • Strength of schedule
  • Quality-win bonus

References

  • Hockey East (2025). DI Men's Ice Hockey Institutes NPI for Championship Selections.
  • USCHO (2025). The NPI is going to replace the PairWise. What does that mean for college hockey?
  • NCAA (2023). Women's hockey championship selection process explained.

KRACH

Men'sWomen's

A Bradley-Terry rating: the model family behind the NCAA committee's PairWise comparison tool.

How it works

Solves for a rating per team so that each team's expected number of wins against its actual opponents, rating_A / (rating_A + rating_B) summed over its games, equals its actual number of wins.

Main attributes

  • Fits the whole season at once
  • Wins and losses only
  • No home-ice or margin terms
  • Rating is a win-odds scale

References

  • Bradley, R. A. & Terry, M. E. (1952). Rank analysis of incomplete block designs: I. The method of paired comparisons. Biometrika, 39(3/4), 324-345.
  • Hunter, D. R. (2004). MM algorithms for generalized Bradley-Terry models. Annals of Statistics, 32(1), 384-406.
  • KRACH explained (overview of Ken Butler's rating)

Massey

Men'sWomen's

A least-squares rating: the set of ratings that best explains every game's goal differential.

How it works

Solves one linear equation per game (winner's rating minus loser's rating ≈ goal margin) for the single set of ratings with the smallest total error. Adds a fitted home-ice term, a rest/fatigue adjustment, and ridge regularization so teams with thin schedules don't get extreme ratings.

Main attributes

  • Fits the whole season at once
  • Uses goal differential
  • Home-ice and rest adjustments
  • Rating is in goals

References

  • Massey, K. (1997). Statistical models applied to the rating of sports teams. Honors thesis, Bluefield College.
  • Langville, A. N. & Meyer, C. D. (2012). Who's #1? The Science of Rating and Ranking. Princeton University Press.

HockeyBT

Men'sWomen's

An extended Bradley-Terry model (Davidson-Beaver) with home ice and ties.

How it works

Uses the same core as KRACH and adds two fitted terms: a home-ice advantage, and a tie parameter so overtime and shootout games count as closer to a draw than a clean win. A prior keeps thinly-scheduled teams from drifting to extreme ratings.

Main attributes

  • Fits the whole season at once
  • Win / tie / loss outcomes
  • Fitted home-ice term
  • Regularized

References

  • Davidson, R. R. (1970). On extending the Bradley-Terry model to accommodate ties in paired comparison experiments. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 65(329), 317-328.
  • Davidson, R. R. & Beaver, R. J. (1977). On extending the Bradley-Terry model to incorporate within-pair order effects. Biometrics, 33(4), 693-702.
  • Wikipedia: Bradley-Terry model

Current strength

These answer “how strong is this team now?” Recent games count more and earlier seasons can inform a team's starting level. They suit predicting upcoming games.

ELO

Men'sWomen's

A sequential rating that updates after every game, like chess ratings.

How it works

Each season starts from a rating carried over from the previous two seasons (mostly last season's), pulled partway back toward the average; a team with no history starts near the low end of the field. After each game the winner takes points from the loser: more for an upset, fewer for an expected win. The exchange is scaled by margin of victory and adjusted for home ice.

Main attributes

  • Updates game by game
  • Uses margin of victory
  • Home-ice adjustment
  • Starts each season from a carried-over rating
  • Order of games matters

References

  • Elo, A. E. (1978). The Rating of Chessplayers, Past and Present. Arco.
  • Hvattum, L. M. & Arntzen, H. (2010). Using ELO ratings for match result prediction in association football. International Journal of Forecasting, 26(3), 460-470.
  • Wikipedia: Elo rating system

Dynamic

Men'sWomen's

A state-space model in which each team's strength changes from week to week.

How it works

Every team has a strength that is allowed to drift a little each week, and that starts each season from last season's closing strength, pulled partway back toward the average. Each game result (regulation win, overtime win, tie, overtime loss, regulation loss) updates the strengths of both teams, with a fitted home-ice term. The rating shown is the most likely strength as of the latest week, fit jointly over this season and the previous two.

Main attributes

  • Strength changes week to week
  • Recent games count more
  • Win / overtime / tie outcomes
  • Fitted home-ice term
  • Starts each season from the previous two seasons

References

  • Glickman, M. E. & Stern, H. S. (1998). A state-space model for National Football League scores. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 93(441), 25-35.
  • Lopez, M. J., Matthews, G. J. & Baumer, B. S. (2018). How often does the best team win? A unified approach to understanding randomness in North American sport. Annals of Applied Statistics, 12(4), 2483-2516.
  • Whelan, J. T. & Klein, A. (2021). Bradley-Terry modeling with multiple game outcomes with applications to college hockey. Preprint.

Model settings

ELO, Massey, HockeyBT and Dynamic each have a few settings. They are not guessed: they were chosen by refitting each model through past seasons and scoring its predictions. Only ELO and Dynamic use earlier seasons to start a season; every other rating on this site comes from the current season's games alone, and earlier seasons were used only to choose the settings.

How they were chosen

For each model, many combinations of settings were tried. Each combination was refit every two weeks through each season from 2021-22 to 2025-26, using only games played before that date, and used to predict the next two weeks of games. Predictions were scored by log loss, which rewards confident correct predictions and penalizes confident wrong ones. Settings whose score could not be told apart from the best were treated as equivalent, and the one closest to the previous value was kept. The choice was then checked by choosing on some seasons and scoring on others. Men's and women's were tuned separately, so a few settings differ. The Dynamic model was tuned the same way, refit weekly. NPI uses the NCAA's published weights, and KRACH has no adjustable settings.

Conference strength

The Conferences pages compare conferences using only games between them, since games within a conference can't show how one conference stacks up against another.

How it works

Each team's conference comes from its conference games on this season's schedule. Teams with no conference games are independents: they count as opponents but aren't ranked as a conference. For each conference the page shows its record, points percentage (a tie counts as half a win) and goal differential in games against other conferences, a conference-vs-conference breakdown, and a home/road/neutral split. Each model column is the average, over every member and every team outside the conference, of that model's chance that the member wins on neutral ice.

Main attributes

  • Cross-conference games only
  • Membership updated each season
  • One rating column per predictive model
  • Most informative after early January
